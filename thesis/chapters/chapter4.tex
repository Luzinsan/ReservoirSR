\chapter{Implementation}
\label{chap:implementation}

\section{Software Stack Overview}
\label{sec:impl_overview}

The software stack supporting the methodology of Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology} is organised around two regimes of operation, sketched in Figure~\ref{fig:stack_overview}. The offline regime trains the super-resolution generators and exports them for inference. It is implemented as a single command-line service that runs three sequential stages: dataset construction with preprocessing and normalisation, training under PyTorch Lightning with Hydra-based configuration, and ONNX export of the resulting model. All persistent artifacts of the offline regime are stored on the file system: the \texttt{.npz} archives produced by the numerical solver, the normalisation constants \texttt{stats.json} estimated on the training subset, and the exported \texttt{.onnx} model. MLflow runs as an independent observer alongside the training stage and records its metrics, hyperparameters, and intermediate checkpoints. The same numerical solver that generates the training corpus in the offline regime is also used at runtime in the online regime: it streams a coarse-grid snapshot directly to the desktop application, which loads the exported \texttt{.onnx} model and the corresponding \texttt{stats.json} from the file system and performs super-resolution inference for visualisation. The remaining sections of this chapter document the three components of this architecture in turn: the CLI service and its configuration in Section~\ref{sec:impl_training}, the ONNX export and the inference path in Section~\ref{sec:impl_inference}, and the desktop application in Section~\ref{sec:impl_app}.
